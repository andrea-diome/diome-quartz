## Highlights
* The difference between screen-based search and voice based search is the difference between a map and an oracle - on screens we’re given a list of search results to navigate, whilst in voice based search we’re not read a list of potential links - we’re given an answer.
* In 2007, Danny Hillis built Freebase, a knowledge graph that scraped data from the emerging networks of web 2.0, mapping the different types of connections between them. By encoding these types of connection within the data, it meant that search engines could do some of the work that users had previously had to do in deciding what result was the best answer.
* Metaweb, the company that built Freebase, was acquired by Google in 2010. As part of the deal, Hillis required Google to keep the knowledge graph public via an API for at least 5 years. In 2015, Google shut down the Freebase API and launched their own Knowledge Base that now runs the ‘knowledge panel’ you see on Google search results.
* Danny Hillis and SJ Klein are working on a new version of Freebase called The Underlay. This updated version is based not just on helping machines understand the links between things, and what those links mean, but also the context of those links - who created them, and whether the links are reliable.
* The hallucinatory responses in ChatGPT are not answers - they are paths within a multidimensional space of knowledge, helping us map out those spaces and understand its hills and valleys.
* Maybe this is what LLMs can do best - map knowledge spaces to reveal their biases and assumptions.
