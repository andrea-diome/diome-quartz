## Highlights
Hyperagents: people with outsized capacity to affect the world, most people who are most oriented to collect power are not looking to share power or consider the needs of the collective, example: Putin, Ghengis Khan
Institutions: not a single agent has power, but due to processes, laws, etc the organization as a whole has outsized power, example: country. Slower to collaborate, institutional decay occurs, so hyperagents can often out-manouver them.
Egregores: Cultural groups or mimetic tribes that coordinate without being an institution in order to exercise power, emergent cultural attractors, example: wokeism, magaism

How do we develop people who have hyperagency capacities but are more aligned with collective and environmental wellbeing? How do we ensure they don't lose the capacity to have that influence due to these considerations?

How do we design institutions that are not obligated to be sociopathic? How do we support institutions in becoming more coherent over time so they are not succeptible to capture?

How do we support the generative emergence of egregores that are not oriented against an outgroup?

How do we bind effectiveness and power with wisdom and good stewardship of power?

Controlling access to computation is the game of power of the 21st century.

Our ability to deploy increasingly powerful technology subsedes our ability to implement effective governance of that power.

Waiting for a super powerful AI to run things is like waiting for the return of the savior.

It's really easy to imagine a world where we have exponential power and blow ourselves up, but not so easy to imagine a world where we govern ourselves effectively considering hyperagents, multipolar traps etc without some kind of AI, genetic enginerring etc.
