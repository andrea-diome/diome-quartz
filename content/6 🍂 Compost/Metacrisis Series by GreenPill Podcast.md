## Part 1: The Metacrisis
[Original Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WVEP0zAK-xQ)
### What is the Metacrisis?
The point of talking about this is to understand it, formulate the right question to get to the right answer.
Identify what needs to happen and by when.
We're overstepping planetary boundaries.
Linear materials economy (creation of waste) connected to an economy requiring exponential growth.
Effects of climate change will cause governmental and supply chain issues that might be catastrophic even before the complete breakdown of climate conditions.
AI risks are also lots of different risks in one:
- becoming intelligent and taking over the world
- creating very negative externalities ie Facebook
- Being used to create weapons (tech 'democratizing everything' also means democratizing the ability to do harm at large scales)
Biotech another risk: people in their garage making bio weapons. (Table top CRISPR)
"Decentralized catastrophe weapons for everyone- not such a good thing."
The metacrisis is essentially about all of these risks together, growing over time.
We have to solve all to survive but only fail at one for catastrophe to occur.
What are the underlying patters? Symptoms of coordination failure.

### Multipolar Traps
- Everyone's overfishing
- No one has the power to protect the fish, only exploit
- The ones who exploit win, so if you don't exploit you lose
When the commons are on a big scale (planetary), and big power is involved, it becomes hard to enforce rules to manage them.
Biggest traps:
- Tragedy of the commons
- Basement arms race
- First mover market advantage (network dynamics leads to natural monopolies). Incentive to avoid externalities, move fast & break things. Risks are socialized, benefits are privatized.

You can't have an enlightened dictator for long because if a corrupt person wants to get into that position, they don't have the contraints of doing the right thing, so more paths to winning. The fight for power itself has a corrupting influence.

### Solving The Metacrisis
Solving the Metacrisis means figuring out an approach to solve Multipolar Traps in general, not just each individually.
Identifying and addressing perverse incentives.
How do we identify negative externalities and internalize them?

### Third Attractor
In complex system, we can't predict the future but we can predict the general flow.
If a raindrop falls on a mountain, we can't predict the flow of each raindrop, but we can say that if it falls on one side of the mountain it will likely end up on the big lake on that side. 
The lake is an attractor ie. general destination of the flow.
In the Metacrisis, the two attractors are:
- Catastrophe: for example, economies keep driving climate change, distributed causes, not bad actors. Additionally, bad actors with basement weapons of mass destruction.
- Dystopia: Surveillance state becomes ubiquitous: prevent basement weapons by surveilling every basement. Global government to control economic externalities needs international agreement, and international enforcement. No one should have this much power, how do you create checks and balances on it?

The third attractor is a world with the necessary levels of coordination to prevent catastrophe, but with the right checks and balances to prevent dystopia.

How can positive virtues be enacted knowing that some people will try to game them? Beyond naivity and cynicism.

Upper left quadrant is where we are going now.
Each catastrophe motivates us enough to accept a dystopic solution. Since the dystopic solution is not a global one, each dystopic system competes in a global arms race, causing more catastrophes.

If we are focused on just solving one catastrophe, it's easy to make others more likely ie. feeding hungry people with degenerative agriculture, or to make dystopias more likely. Have to think on a systemic level to avoid creating unwanted externalities and putting in checks and balances of power. We define problems too narrowly right now.

Market growth = energy use
Renewables helping but not getting even close (they also take energy to make)
Diminishing returns on fossil fuels because we've gone through the easy stuff
Exponential demand for growth and fuel
Making fuel the real cost (extracting CO2 from atmosphere, cleaning up pollution) it would be so expensive the global economy would collapse
Trying to solve climate change means going up against the whole global economy

If you can force transparency, you have a hope at enforcement. Classification of information drives breakdown of democracy. Driving transparency supports race to the top.

In a situation where AI takes almost all jobs, humans could focus on highly rewarding work like providing high-class care and developing future thinkers. Can kids have the smartest thinkers of all time be their tutors via AI?

"Tech has to facilitate what is uniquely human."

AI helping to understand people's values to support better governance.

What if Facebook corrected for your biases and connected you with people that could expand your network?

If you think that a negative future is set, then you might take actions to protect from that future that have a negative effect in the present. A strange variation of the ends justify the means.

"We have to build a technosphere compatible with the biosphere"

Having faith the the solution lies in the unknown unknowns.

## Part 2: Superstructures
[Original Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WRohQKNNuM4)
### Governance Considerations 
"What is the tech that would create a social system that would regulate the rest of the tech?"
Tech affects psychologies affect cultures affect social systems
Technology is how we act on the world: increases our capacity to take action.
The process of evolution selects means that sensing and actuating are tied together: no point in sensing a danger you can't act to avoid, or taking an action that has no meaning to your perceived reality. 
If our capacity to act evolves, our sensing capacity adapts to pay attention to the actions we can take.
If you're walking around the forest with a chainsaw vs climbing shoes, trees hold a different meaning because you can take different actions towards them.
"There is no Metacrisis without tech." You can't destroy the world with rocks. Tech is extending our capacity for destruction.
All tech design is values-based. 
Externality = cost outside of the balance sheet = stealing from the commons.
1. Physical externality: damage to human or planetary bodies
2. Psychosocial externality: incentivize humans to act differently to get the advantages of using the tech. Changing behavior changes the human mind.
"You can literally breed narcissism and body dysmorphia in the whole population through the design of the tech."
Printing press ended feudalism by spreading knowledge.
Plough = grow more crops with less humans = more available calories = population growth
If you are not using it, your community is at a disadvantage because less people to protect it against enemy forces. Therefore, it becomes table stakes.
Values also change: you can't respect the value of the life of an ox if you have to beat it all day.
From animals are brothers to man's dominion over nature.
From men and women are equal to man is the boss because he can operate the plough.
From few possessions to storing and hoarding.
Hiearchical value systems emerge.
Culture shapes tech, tech shapes culture.
Technosolutionist capitalism is spread by the winners of the current system in the same way Christinaity was promoted by Kings become rich through the crusades
Dominant narrative a trope for the dominant power structure
Avoid ideology of winners by getting everyone in the conversation (humans & more-than-human)
We can also use law and regulation to codify values to avoid bad things being done with tech.
New tech tends to be decentralizing at first but then centralizing over time as the winners keep winning (similar to economic models of exchange > early winners tend to win more over time)

3 layers to civilization:
- Infrastructure: Tech stack that helps get things done. From spears and baskets to ploughs and fences to computers.
- Social structure: Governance & laws
- Superstructure: Values, religion, spiritual beliefs, ideology, what is good?
All affect each other
Need to codify positive values in tech that then codify positive values in the social structure
Tech that mediates the social structure:
We learn belief systems by what we are surrounded by.
If the incentive is to commodify people to extract profit, then the tech will amplify the things that better commodify the user: emotions over rationality, being part of a bounded group over social cohesion.
You can't opt out if you can't be effective in society if not engaging.
If the asymmetry of power is too great, you can't adequately consent. 
If a platform has enough data on you they can predict your behavior better than your spouse, they have to be a fiduciary because they have control over your decisions due to their knowledge, like a doctor in a health situation.
We're extracting from human minds the behavior predispositions that support the extension of power. People need be able to provide true consent to "AIs being pointed at their mind."
1. How do we develop people with complex superstructures ie. high capacity for sense-making and decision-making
2. How do we harness those capacities to create social structures that are then going to govern tech?
Infrastructure has advanced at hyperspeed but the same level of change has not reached social structures or superstructures.

### Conflict vs Mistake Theory
Externality = mistake, not a problem created on purpose
Conflict = they do it on purpose
Conflict theory: what things create human conflict and how could they be addressed better.
The acknowledgement that mistakes can be made can be used as a cop out: if later on I can say it was an unpredictable mistake, I can ignore the problem. Plausible deniability.
We might not be able to predict all of the effects and externalities of tech, but we can do a way better job than we're doing now just through known frameworks and experimentation. You also need to build in governance that enables continuous observation and course correction.
However, the incentive is to not do that because first to market wins, so slow and internalizing risks loses.
Where does this technology enable people to get an advantage over others? Where does this technology affect value systems and thinking? How does it increase conflict?
How to change incentives:
- Attribute externalities and make pay for costs (unprofitable)
- Create governance protocol first, before tech is rolled out has to pass risk analysis (like drugs)
Disruption, move fast & break things: narrative that excuses breaking the social contract to privatize gains and socialize losses

Important decisions can't be made through numbers, they have to be made through qualitative assessment, earnestness, values, to be valid.

Less decisions made through incentives, more through intrinsic motivation around what is meaningful. Less "gamification" more doing it because it matters.

Wide distribution of human sensors means better, more meaningful data, means a better system. 
Decentralize design of systems, identification of externalities and solution ideas.

## Part 3: MetacrisisDAO
[Original Link](https://youtu.be/hv_xBK_XZjw)
A problem well understood is half addressed
We have the power to shape the implementation of cryptographic tech now to avoid negative externalities.
There's an inherent between narrowing the problem space and seeing the big picture, how to focus on a part while still keeping an eye on the whole? Step out of the usual space of consideration.
The relationship between AI & web3 is important: the incentives are high to produce personalized propaganda at mass scale, how do you understand what is real?
Can be for personalized ed but incentives not as strong (AI spirit animals)

Questions:
- Thinking about the entire field of Web3 (taxonomy?): what are all the positive and negative externalities that crypto can affect or create? Example: the gene sequence of a deadly pathogen is on the blockchain and now anyone can print it in their garage. How can you take it offline or provide levels of access? (Futures wheel)
- By topic (planetary boundaries, coral etc), how would web3 in combo with other tech and processes provide new solutions? (Systems map)
- How could systems be put in place to advance governance systems?

Questions as summarized by Kevin Owocki:
When exploring a problem and/or a possible solution
-   Why
    -   What values are being served by this solution (ie, what values do I see as being harmed or underserved by this problem)?
    -   Which values are in a dialectic relationship with the values stated above?
    -   What are the perceived tensions or tradeoffs between the seemingly opposing values?
    -   How could the pairs of values be seen as complementary and interdependent rather than opposing? How could trade-offs be replaced with synergistic satisfiers?
    -   Are there any new values that matter to factor the balance or integration of the values pairs?
    -   How does factoring this wider set of interconnecting values affect the problem definition? How would the solution have to be updated to better serve all the related and affected values?
    -   Are there social traps involved in driving this problem?
    -   Are there perverse incentives or systemic choice architectures contributing to it?
-   Who
    -   Who is the problem harming/ who is the solution intended to benefit?
    -   Who else might be affected by the full set of activities needed to enact this solution (including opportunity cost)?
    -   Who doesn’t/ won’t like this solution and why? What values do they see it as harming and/or what facts do they see it as misassessing and/or what alternatives do they prefer and why?
    -   What might the effects of polarizing those people against this solution be? What would it take to modify the proposed solution in ways that meet the primary initial goals plus sufficiently meet these people’s goals so that they don’t oppose it?
    -   Who is close to (various aspects of) this problem that might have knowledge or insights that should be involved in the solution-informing process?
    -   Where are old tribal identities contributing to conflict about this solution that actually has little to do with this solution? What would it take to bring these people into more shared relationality?
    -   Who has perverse incentives that benefit from this problem or are in conflict with the proposed solution?
-   What
    -   What are the relevant facts pertaining to this issue?
    -   What is our basis for confidence and degree of confidence in these facts?
    -   Which other facts that might also be relevant are missing?
    -   How else could these same facts be interpreted?
    -   Where are different camps emphasizing different facts? Where are they in disagreement about the interpretation of facts? Where do they disagree on the veracity or confidence of a fact? Which of these are the source of the disagreement? What would it take to bring both parties to a shared perception or to make the disagreement less consequential?
    -   What are the primary causes of this problem? How do we know?
    -   What issues are upstream from this issue and affect it? Are all those elements factored into the solution? What might other causes be?
    -   What issues are downstream from this issue and are affected by it? What externalities (positive and negative, physical and psychosocial) might result from this solution being enacted?
    -   What nth-order effects of this solution might different specialists or people with different experience sets see that I can’t?
    -   What are the effects on the whole ecology of not enacting a solution?
    -   What is the total scale of (the class of) this issue, and the timelines associated with it? Can this solution address the scale and timelines?
    -   How important is this issue relative to other issues that could receive the same time and resources?
    -   What assumptions about the problem framing might be worth questioning?
-   How
    -   How could I see this problem totally differently?
    -   What are totally different ways people could frame this problem?
    -   What does it look like through the lens of various contexts (eg, culture, technology, economics, history, art, politics, etc)?
    -   How could different epistemologies be employed to assess this situation (eg, developmental psychology, game theory, complexity science, education, cultural materialism, etc.)
    -   How might new possibilities emerge that we can’t currently think of (eg, question assumptions, bring more resources to bear, create new partnerships, resolve rivalries, think at different scales, engage more perspectives, create new incentives, etc.)
    -   How would I know if the solution was creating externalities in areas I didn’t know to predict? What type of monitoring/listening do I want to include in the strategy rollout to learn about unanticipated effects quickly?
    -   How would I set up the governance of this solution such that it could factor info about unanticipated effects or changing contexts quickly and effectively?
