## Highlights

Indigenous couple is challenging what AI could be by preserving the Maori language through collaborative data ownership

#### Opportunities & Insights
"models built by averaging data from entire populations have sidelined minority and marginalized communities even as they are disproportionately subjected to the technology’s impacts. “Data is the last frontier of colonization” The open-source, free-wheeling culture of data science is often antithetical to the practice of data sovereignty, as is the culture of AI. Creating and enforcing values based contracts to access data"

#### Indigenous & Community Knowledge
"Companies have relentlessly mined people for their faces, voices, and behaviors to enrich bottom lines." 
"Over the last decade, AI researchers have pushed the field to new limits with the dogma “More is more”: Amass more data to produce bigger models (algorithms trained on said data) to produce better results." 
"Global AI development is impoverishing communities and countries that don’t have a say in its development—the same communities and countries already impoverished by former colonial empires." 
"large language models with powerful autocomplete and text analysis used in search, email, and social media are also accelerating language loss, in the same way colonization and assimilation policies did previously. Relying on such services in daily work and life  coerces some communities to speak dominant languages instead of their own.","https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/05/20/1025135/ai-large-language-models-bigscience-project/" 
"""Regardless of how widely spoken they are, languages belong to a people.” Kathleen Siminyu" 
"He wanted to create a tool that would display a transcription alongside each [Maori elder] interview. Te reo [Maori language] learners would then be able to hover on words and expressions to see their definitions." 

#### Regenerative Digital Spaces
"Problem: To build a te reo speech recognition model, it needed an abundance of transcribed audio. To transcribe the audio, it needed the advanced speakers whose small numbers it was trying to compensate for in the first place." 
"Approach: rather than transcribe existing audio, they would ask people to record themselves reading a series of sentences designed to capture the full range of sounds in the language. From those thousands of pairs of spoken and written sentences, it would learn to recognize te reo syllables in audio." 
"If they want te reo to truly be ubiquitous—to the point of having te reo–speaking voice assistants on iPhones and Androids—they’ll need to partner with big tech companies." 
"It created a data license that spells out the ground rules for future collaborations based on the Māori principle of kaitiakitanga, or guardianship. It will only grant data access to organizations that agree to respect Māori values, stay within the bounds of consent, and pass on any benefits derived from its use back to the Māori people. there remain questions around its enforceability." 
"Mozilla’s Siminyu says it’s easy for individuals and communities to be disenfranchised; value is seen to come not from the people who give their data but from the ones who take it away. “They say, ‘Your voice isn’t worth anything on its own. It actually needs us, someone with a capacity to bring billions together, for each to be meaningful,’” she says."
