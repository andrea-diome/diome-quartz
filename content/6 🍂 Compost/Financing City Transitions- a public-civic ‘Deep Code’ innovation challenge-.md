## Highlights
* For example, we are looking for ways to test new public / civic financial instruments in the context of retrofitting a neighbourhood via a targeted Central Bank backed bond. Or, in demonstrating the entangled value of a city-wide tree canopy, we aspire to create a multicontextual business model which represents the intrinsic value of different types of capital in their own rights (e.g. social, natural, human) rather than converting them to their monetary equivalents.
* How can we design systemic business plans and models?
* Our ambition is to create an open source modular canvas with a dual function: Firstly, cities will need to make the case for portfolio scale investments that span multiple classes of assets, liabilities, timeframes and spheres of risk. Secondly, robust, understandable data will be required to provide the foundations for the multi-actor agreements necessary to execute systemic business models.
* What are the system-level agreement structures that will be needed to coordinate value flows between the multiple beneficiaries and liability owners?
* In our #Beyond the Rules work we have started to look at this in the context of many-to-many funding agreements
* What kind of new institutions will be needed to hold the ‘treasury’ function of a city?
* This can only happen if the liabilities, benefits, risks and mitigation strategies are financed and orchestrated from a point of shared intention. This will require examining the dynamics that drive the plurality of incentives and motivations for investment and designing tools and governance structures to align and coordinate them. For example, what is driving the mismatch between the available EU Funding and the transition projects struggling to raise capital?
* Incorporating risk and resilience value into accounting models: what are the pathways for intersecting tech and/or legal innovations with accounting models to shift incentives?
* if enterprises operated under a revocable social licence that was linked to real time data, the city treasury could restrict funding if the licence was revoked.
* What would a long-term inflationary landscape mean in terms of pricing and valuation structures?
* If we don’t start to honour non-financial forms of capital now in our value systems and measurements, the potential for rising prices to further compound existing inequalities is profound and systemic.
* How can we nest different theories of change within single point system demonstrators?
* in the context of business plans, what if profit was reframed from a measure of extractive accumulation to a signal of value flow? How would this manifest in the collective economic goals of a city?
* How can we ground macroeconomic policy in the practical context of the emerging city-level transitions?
* Horizon 3 aspirations: What are the strands of technology that exist (even if unrelated), that if coordinated and scaled, could drive the distribution of capital production at the city level in the future?
* In our current systems, we have effectively outsourced societal decision making and theories of value to centralised capital production of banks. The exceptional power bestowed on commercial banks to issue credit fundamentally determines which activities and sectors of society receive funding and thus their viability. In a regenerative future, this dynamic will need to be reimagined. Such an evolution could take the form of decentralised civic financing – via smart asset backed bills of exchange, or it might emerge from the realm of the adjacent possible,
* how do we finance the future – not based on the predictive capacity of capital allocators, but instead allocate to support the generative discovery of value led by communities.
