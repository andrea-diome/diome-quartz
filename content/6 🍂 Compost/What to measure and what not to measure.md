## Highlights
* people are fearful and distrustful of data like never before. Yet any path towards a fundamentally better world requires more data and more measurement.
* If I look back on what measurements work well, there are two criteria. One, that there's an objective reality that has been measured, a kind of consensus, and there's a process around coming to that consensus between different stakeholders.
* So, for example, carbon emissions. If carbon emissions get out of control they will have a detrimental effect on the climate so it's worth measuring carbon.
* The second is that good measurements drive action. They're things that enable some kind of possibility to happen.
* Every time I decide to measure something that process reveals what my values are. Why am I measuring this thing? What do I anticipate the outcome of this measurement will be? Measurement is just the first step in a process of understanding. What’s really valuable is our analysis of that data and how we derive meaning from it.
* We have more information that we can use to measure success, but it's not being thought of like that. It's still being thought of as how much revenue and growth we can have.
* Creating a marketplace of debate around both the metrics and the analysis feels like one of the more concrete things that will help.
* we're sitting here optimizing for the map when we're not really dealing with the territory.
* We have a desire to measure things in ever-shorter timespans, but the important phenomena we’re trying to measure are often inherently slow-moving.
* Some of the least valid data sources we've tested are the ones that are based on real-time sentiment analysis.
* There's such a hunger and a desire to measure in these infinitesimally small time slices that it generates a tremendous amount of bullshit. We end up actually measuring the wrong things in an effort to be fast.
* Thinking about the maturity levels of metrics and whether there could even be a metric of metrics. Like, this is a metric that we feel very confident in versus one that’s in development or beta,
* So if they do understand, why can't they have a choice of being able to tweak the things? “For this period I'm just going to learn a skill or I want to slow down this addictive mechanic.”
