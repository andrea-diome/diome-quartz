## Highlights
* Within the enormous project of shifting away from the ingrained norms of capitalist individualism and towards interdependence, a prevalent pathway is that of worldbuilding:
* while the implementation of new worlds into lived realities happens over vast time and space, where can working towards this be materialized in the day-to-day? A guiding praxis for this might be the ancient technology of ritual;
* “Rituals are symbolic acts. They represent, and pass on, the values and orders on which a community is based”(Byung-Chul Han).
* Existing in a designated realm of enchantment, ritual allows for imagining avenues outside of the limited arenas of knowledge and possibility constructed within western capitalist society. Enchantment itself has underlying connotations of magic, a space intrinsically dedicated to a reimagining outside of a conventional “reality.”
* It is the repetition of a specific practice, and this process-focused repetition is what can make it a continual catalyst of change.
* Ritual has not been diminishing with western modernity but rather recontextualized within capitalist agendas.
* The financial market consists of daily arbitrary assignments of value, and through these repetitions, that value is made real. When creating market values, actual future income is unknown, but the repetitive practice creates normalcy defining a value, and this market value then influences decision-making around mass consumption.
* If these ritualistic modalities can be effective on such a global scale—causing discrimination, exploitation, and extraction—what forms of ritual can undo these effects for the betterment of the earth and its inhabitants?
* To materialize the modalities of meaning-making, we must locate the places where they convene with intentional praxis, and how to orient ourselves in those locations towards ecosocial renewal.
* Humans are creatures of habit and ritual praxis can have a reprogramming effect on the habitual psyche due to its underlying form of repetitive attentiveness.
* Capitalism has spread the narratives of individualistic separation of oneself from one’s environment in order to prioritize the body as a force of labor.
* Continually holding awareness and reverence for the subtle needs of a place, whether that place is the individual body or the earth, can move beyond both purely individualist and purely collectivist assumptions.
