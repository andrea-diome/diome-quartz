## How to organize it
Because digital gardens focus on building in public, it's important to show how complete or not the note is. It's also important to keep it organized, so it's easy to find the information again.

### How [Maggie](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history)does it
Indicating how "ready" the idea is, or it's [[Epistemic Status]]:
-   🌱 _Seedlings_ for rough early ideas
-   🌿 _Budding_ for ideas she's spent some time working through
-   🌳 _Evergreen_ for fully-fledged ideas that still get tended over time
Other tags and metadata:
- She includes the dates she _planted_ and _last tended_ each item for visitors to understand how long or recently it has been active.
- She also puts tags based on the topics related to that notes, for example, "Digital Gardening" & "The Web"
- She categorizes the format of the piece in "Essays", "Notes" & "Patters"
	- Notes are just "unopinionated" scribblings on topics she doesn't necessarily understand
	- Essays are more long form and have a clear "agenda" or point of view
	- Patters are design elements from around the web

![[Screenshot of Maggie Appleton's Website Tags.png]]

### How [Devon](https://devonzuegel.com/post/epistemic-statuses-are-lazy-and-that-is-a-good-thing) does it
Devon takes a more qualitative approach, using [[Epistemic Status]] and Epistemic Effort sections to explain in some detail the frame of thought behind the piece. 

![[Screenshot of Devon Zuegel's epistemic status.png]]

### How [Gwern](https://gwern.net/About#information-organizing) does it
They have a complex system which indicates how complete it is, how confident they are in the piece of writing (8 different states from certain to impossible), and how "life changing" it is on a scale of 1-10. "Similar" takes the visitor to a list of related links, both internal and external, and "bibliography" to a list of the links referenced in the article.

When clicking on a topic tag, the visitor is taken to [a page with links](https://gwern.net/docs/insight-porn/index) relating to that topic, both internal and external.

![[Screenshot of Gwern's website tags.png]]

### How [Hypha](https://digitalgarden.hypha.coop/) does it
When visiting the site, an entry is chosen at random to display. There's a map of the contents in the background, but there doesn't seem to be a way to view just the map.

In the entry itself, the metadata displays when the post was created and updated. Each post has a plant-themed icon, but it's not clear what they represent. The content of each entry consists primarily of quotes and their corresponding links. At the bottom of the entry, there is a section for internal links that reference the note.
![[Screenshot of Hydra Coop's Digital Garden.png]]
Links include a preview of the linked page.
