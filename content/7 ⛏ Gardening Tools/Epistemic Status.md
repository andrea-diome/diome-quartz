Epistemology is the study of knowledge, therefore, epistemic status refers to the backstory of how knowledge came to be. An example of an epistemic status is "Very speculative", or "Budding" vs "Evergreen", or even have a numerical confidence rating. These tags can be used in #DigitalGardens 

Mentioned in [[Information architecture of digital gardens]]

Adding an epistemic status adds context to a piece of content, which is something that is very badly needed in the current internet, as mentioned in Jenny Odell's [[How To Do Nothing - Resisting the Attention Economy]]

It also showcases the process of thinking itself.

It can also be helpful in giving the writer leeway to publish ideas that might seem wild, or they are not 100% in agreement with.

> A few years ago I wrote [a piece about Singapore](https://devonzuegel.com/post/singapore-sovereign-city) claiming that totalitarian governments at a very small scale may actually make sense. It was well-written and researched, but I was also very unsure of the ultimate claim... So it sat in my folder for years, because I didn't feel comfortable publishing something that I wasn't very confident about... I felt fine sharing the concept in the context of the conversation because I could clarify my lack of confidence in the claim. But of course there was nothing stopping me from doing exactly the same thing on my own blog! Why not just state that in my written piece as well?!

From [Devon Zugel's digital garden](https://devonzuegel.com/post/epistemic-statuses-are-lazy-and-that-is-a-good-thing), which is an example of what this looks like in practice.

A good explanation of this topic, as well as another great example, can be found on [Maggie Appleton's website](https://maggieappleton.com/epistemic-disclosure)
