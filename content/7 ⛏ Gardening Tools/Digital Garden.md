## What is a digital garden
A website in which content is organized by topic rather than time posted, and linked by contextual association. The posts are intended to be a perpetual work-in-progress and grow over time, rather than being complete when they are published, as with a traditional blog

## Why digital gardens
- Digital gardens are less about performance and more about letting ideas grow.
- Digital gardens allow you to [Learn In Public](https://www.swyx.io/learn-in-public/), making connections with others' work and allowing others to find your ideas.
- It allows you to learn faster and more deeply.
- It builds an internet of communal knowledge, rather than immediate incendiary content. 
- It encourages intercropping of ideas, rather than monocropping (reminiscent of [[How To Do Nothing - Resisting the Attention Economy]])
- Avoids walled gardens, where your content makes Silicon Valley rich, in favor of ownership and interoperability.

## History of digital gardens
The earliest mention of the term is in Mark Bernstein's [Hypertext Gardens](http://www.eastgate.com/garden/Enter.html), published in 1998. 

The topic dies off for a while, before returning in Mike Caufield's keynote [The Garden and the Stream: a Technopastoral](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ckv_CjyKyZY&feature=emb_logo) which later became [an essay](https://hapgood.us/2015/10/17/the-garden-and-the-stream-a-technopastoral/)

Early internet creators envisioned it as a forest garden full of paths to explore, semi-wild but tended. However, we've all been caught up in streams: context-light short-form thoughts that focus on the immediate. This reminds me of the idea of [[Being involved in different levels of information]].

Some other important links here are:
- Tom Critchlow's 2018 article [Of Digital Streams, Campfires and Gardens](https://tomcritchlow.com/2018/10/10/of-gardens-and-wikis/) #toread
- Amy Hoy's [How the Blog Broke the Web](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-blogs-broke-the-web/) #toread which sounds somewhat similar to [On Platform Design](https://so-far.xyz/issue/on-platform-design-part-i-from-subject-centred-communities-to-persona-driven-platforms)

[Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history) does a great job of summarizing the history of the topic.
