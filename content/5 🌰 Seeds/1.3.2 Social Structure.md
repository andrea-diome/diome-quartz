## Overview
### What is it?
Social structure refers to the organized set of [[Institutions]], norms, and relationships that shape and regulate the behavior and interactions of individuals within a society. Governance, the processes, systems, mechanisms and relationships through which decisions are made, are core to social structures. 
### Why is it important?
- Social structures shape the norms that govern how we relate to each other and the planetary ecosystem.
- They determine the power dynamics, decision-making processes, and resource allocation that can either perpetuate unsustainable practices or enable regenerative ones.
- Existing social structures often enshrine anthropocentric values, hierarchical power structures, and extractive economic models that are fundamentally at odds with ecological balance and regeneration.
- Transitioning towards a regenerative paradigm requires transforming the underlying social structures that have entrenched unsustainable practices and worldviews.
### How does it need to change?
- While money and markets provide a decentralized system for large-scale choice-making, the current structures concentrate power and prioritize the desires of the wealthy, often at the expense of wider social and ecological considerations ([[Externalities]]).
- We need to reimagine economic and social systems not as separate from, but as integral parts of the wider planetary community and biosphere. 
- Instead of perpetuating the extractive and growth-centric paradigm, new social structures must be designed around the core value of creating the conditions for all life to flourish.
- Choice-making processes must account for the long-term well-being of the entire planetary ecosystem, incorporating feedback loops that help course-correct when human activities disrupt ecological or social stability.
- Social structures must move away from increasingly centralized control towards models of governance that give voice to all stakeholders, including future generations and non-human entities.
