## Hardness
The concept of "hardness" introduced in Josh Stark's article, [Atoms, Institutions, Blockchains](https://stark.mirror.xyz/n2UpRqwdf7yjuiPKVICPpGoUNeDhlWxGqjulrlpyYi0), refers to the ability to create fixed, reliable points across time that facilitate coordination and predictability at scale. Without such stability, essential societal mechanisms like money, trade, and legal commitments would become unmanageably risky due to uncertainty about their future.

Hardness is always about a "cast"—which is essentially a claim or statement about the future. The source of a cast's hardness (atoms, institutions, or blockchains) determines its reliability:

1. **Atoms**: Physical realities of the universe, including matter and physical laws, which offer a form of hardness that is automatic and not subject to human discretion. An example is the limited supply of gold on Earth, which can be relied upon for commerce due to its difficulty to alter.
2. **[[Institutions]]**: This form of hardness is based on the collective behavior and agreements within human-made systems. The legal system, especially contract law, serves as an instance of institutional hardness.
3. **[[Blockchains]]**: provides hardness through the use of cryptography, networked software, and incentivized human behavior. 

He argues that institutions are adaptable sources of hardness that can create specific, complex rules and agreements that cater to a wide variety of societal needs, unlike the laws of nature which are more or less fixed. However, institutions are made of people, who can be biased, corrupt, or make mistakes, and are mostly limited by national borders, leading to complexities and inefficiencies in global coordination. 

Blockchains can provide a new source of hardness that escapes these limitations (but of course have different limitations themselves).
## Protocols & Communities
The article [Seeing like a protocol](https://barnabe.substack.com/p/seeing-like-a-protocol) by Barnabé Monnot explores the concept of protocol credibility, focusing on the Ethereum blockchain, and examines its interaction with the broader community, including validators, users, and other stakeholders. To make it easier to compare to the idea of harness presented above, we can read 'protocols' or 'the Ethereum blockchain' as blockchains and 'the community' as an organizational institution, containing a number of institutions within itself.

His argument can be summed up as follows:

> *"Protocol credibility = Protocol introspection + Protocol agency + Defence of last resort by the community"*

Where protocol introspection is the visibility or legibility the protocol has of its own operations, and protocol agency is its ability to act when a fault is detected.

> *"The protocol itself functions as a commitment device, embodying the will of the community. **The act of placing agency at protocol-level, rather than in the hands of the community, is how credibility is created."***

In other words, blockchains are a codified representation of the values and goals of the institution. Blockchains enforce the will institutions, and the institutions enforce the values encoded in blockchains.

> *"In this framing, there is no room for a Platonic view of protocol development, where we always move further towards an idealised shape, perhaps what Bitcoin is chasing after. In contrast, protocol development is informed by the political economy of institutions in its environment, where the protocol is one among an ever-expanding web of many other institutions."*
## Evolution of Institutions
In [The Nature of Technology: What It Is and How It Evolves](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Nature-of-Technology/W-Brian-Arthur/9781416544067), W. Brian Arthur defines a framework for understanding the evolution of technology (explored in [[Technology Definitions#Evolution of Technology]]) comprising of 3 key elements:

1. Modular Combination
2. Harnessing of Phenomena
3. Standardization of Components

Could it be that Blockchains, rather than being a *new* source of hardness, are in fact some form of modularization or standardization of components of [[Institutions]], which allows them to better harness phenomena such as human behavior via mechanisms such as incentives? 

This idea is a bit of a jump from the above analyses. Some further work is needed to explain how blockchains act as institutional components. However, I think there is something here which would go some way towards explaining the explosion of institutional evolution and experimentation within the blockchain space.
