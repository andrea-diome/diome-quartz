Blockchain is a decentralized, distributed ledger technology that records transactions across multiple computers in a way that ensures the integrity and security of the data. 
## How they work
A blockchain consists of a series of blocks, each containing a list of transactions. Each block is connected to the previous one through a cryptographic hash, forming a chain. This structure ensures that once a block is added to the chain, its transactions cannot be altered without changing all subsequent blocks, which requires consensus among the network participants. This makes blockchains highly resistant to tampering and fraud.

![[BTC-Mining_ProcessEN-1-1024x610.jpg]]

The decentralization aspect means that the ledger is not controlled by a single entity (like a bank or government), but is instead maintained by a network of nodes (computers). Each node has a copy of the entire blockchain, and transactions are verified through a consensus mechanism, such as proof of work or proof of stake, before being added to the blockchain.
## Usage & Affordances
The technology is best known for its role in supporting cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, and its wider applications include supply chain management, secure voting systems, identity verification, smart contracts, and more. Its key features—decentralization, transparency, and immutability—offer significant benefits in terms of security, trust, and efficiency.
