## Overview
### What is it?
Infrastructure refers to the integrated networks of physical components, organizational processes, and information flows that provision society's essential goods and services. It represents the material and technological foundation enabling human activities and mediating our interactions with the planetary ecosystem.
### Why is it important?
- Infrastructure represents the material and technological base that shapes human interactions with the environment and enables specific modes of production, resource allocation, and economic activities. 
- The forms and functions of infrastructure embody the underlying assumptions and beliefs about our relationship with the natural world.
- Infrastructure plays a crucial role in determining the human-ecosystem relationship and the material conditions that underpin a society's cultural superstructure and social structure.
### How does it need to change?
- To embody regenerative principles, infrastructure must undergo a paradigm shift from linear, extraction-based models towards a worldview of integration and reciprocity within ecological systems.
- Regenerative infrastructures should be rooted in an ethos where the flourishing of one contributes to the flourishing of all, aligning interests and incentives towards collaborative enrichment rather than rivalrous contestation.
