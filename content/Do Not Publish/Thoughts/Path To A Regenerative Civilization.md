## A Regenerative Civilization
### What is a regenerative civilization?
- sits within an ecosystem and coevolves with it
	- deep understanding of living system interrelationships
	- from a fragmented worldview to a whole systems mental model
	- careful observations and understandings of nature shape cultures over time. How do we observe? What do we observe? How do we form understanding of nature and ecosystems? What do we measure?
	- (how does this relate to how we make devices for the human body?)
- builds social capital
	- aims to restore, renew, and revitalize institutions and humans
	- cultural narratives and institutions guide actions towards nature
- internalizies externalities
	- aims to restore, renew, and revitalize its own sources of energy and materials.
- importance of various forms of capital
	- natural capital (ecosystem goods and services), 
	- social capital (relations of trust, norms, obligations, and institutions), 
	- human capital (knowledge, skills, and capabilities),
	- physical and financial capital (infrastructure and financial resources).

### How is our current civilization not regenerative?
-  The current systems of commerce, building, society, geology, and nature are fragmented and not viewed as one system of integrated relationships, which is necessary for a regenerative culture (Note #2).
	- The current mindset is more focused on exploitation and extraction as the core approaches to business and economic growth, rather than focusing on regenerative practices (Note #1).
- Our current economy is extractive, where economic activities destroy living systems. This is because actions in an extractive economy create negative externalities, or damage that is shared by society but does not significantly impact the economic success of those responsible (Note #3).
	- The current market economy has been criticized for its failure to prevent the destruction of nature, instead organizing it along market lines. This includes mechanisms such as trading biodiversity credits or carbon credits, which do not prevent the destruction of nature (Note #5).
- The majority of the world's population is exploited for the gains of a small percentage, leading to a lack of equity and sustainability, and certainly no regeneration (Note #1).
