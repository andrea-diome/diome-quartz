I've recently been working on an app with the working title [Sensemaking Networks](https://diome.xyz/3+%F0%9F%8C%BC+Flowers/Sensemaking+Networks).  The user-facing app is a tool that converts scientists' tweets into [nanopublications](https://diome.xyz/2+%F0%9F%8C%BF+Leaves/Nanopublications). You can check out the [current design prototype here](https://www.figma.com/proto/l9cIbl8xi2JE480oTA85o6/SenseNets-AutoPoster?type=design&node-id=67-4563&t=H1cahFVytfUeydRQ-1&scaling=min-zoom&page-id=0%3A1&starting-point-node-id=67%3A4563&show-proto-sidebar=1&mode=design). 

Over the last few days I've been designing it, the thought has been growing in my head that there is a strong connection between this open science data space, including [Knowledge Space](https://gist.github.com/tkuhn/a3842683b810f1e0dbc72a021083ea31), and the Negation Game. 

The Negation Game is essentially a series of assertions or knowledge records with a particular relationship between them, displayed in a way that supports the understanding of those relationships.

I think this is important because at the moment, users have to manually 'source' (from their own brain or the internet) and then input assertions into the app. It would be interesting if they could draw from a pool of pre-existing data like nanopublications or knowledge records that might be backed up with scientific (or other solid) data, or publish their own assertions as knowledge records, with contextual links.

This idea took me down a rabbit hole of the misinformation and fact-checking landscape. Here are some of the things I came across:

- [Politifact](https://www.politifact.com/): a fact checking platform.
![[Captura de Pantalla 2024-04-19 a la(s) 3.44.53 p. m..png]]
- [Public Editor](https://www.publiceditor.io/) by [Goodly Labs](https://www.goodlylabs.org/): a collective intelligence system that labels specific reasoning mistakes in the daily news, so we all learn to avoid biased thinking.
- [Rbutr](http://rbutr.com/): tells you when the webpage you are viewing has been disputed, rebutted or contradicted elsewhere on the internet.

Each assertion here ie “BREAKING: Russian President Vladimir Putin has declared that Russia will support Iran if the United States attacks Iran’s soil in support of Israel.” is a knowledge record, and the fact-check is a linked knowledge record.

I came to the conclusion that the Negation Game is something like an asynchronous deliberation platform. I think this is important because:

> *"One of the main barriers to deliberation is the complexity of the process, which makes it difficult to scale and reproduce online. Our digital tools are usually designed for participation: they aim to reach large numbers of citizens and are geared towards action (ie a vote), but don’t allow meaningful in-depth debate."*
> [What’s the difference between deliberation and participation?](https://www.citizenlab.co/blog/civic-engagement/whats-the-difference-between-deliberative-and-participatory-democracy/)

So what am I trying to get at here? Not 100% sure but I think its something like: 

1. It would be great to have something like nanopublications/knowledge records for civic data. These could be created from existing laws, speeches by politicians, journalists, fact-checkers, experiences of regular humans, etc with varying degrees of trust attached.
2. Each record could include metadata about the argument for example premises, conclusions, frames, values, stance towards topic and concepts (framework from [PAKT: Perspectivized Argumentation Knowledge Graph and Tool for Deliberation Analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10570.pdf)). These could be created by AI like the SenseNet app is doing with the scientific publications.
3. These records could them be harnessed to create applications on top like the Negation Game or Kialo.
4. These records/apps can be used to have better deliberations and decision-making processes, because you would have the knowledge records backed by evidence or various arguments to build on.
5. As more deliberations happen, more links would be made between records, increasing data quality and usefulness.
6. These deliberations & accompanying knowledge records could potentially support political lobbying in the current world.
