When I presented Diome at Life Itself, Rufus Pollock responded that although each Trunk is important in systems change, the superstructure always came first. He wrote about this idea in an essay called [The Primacy of Being](https://lifeitself.org/primacy-of-being). Increasingly, I believe this to be true.

I've been watching a lot of videos recently, such as [Hegemonitis: Why The West Has Become So Dumb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=32uZmnrxtcg "Hegemonitis: Why The West Has Become So Dumb | Professor Glenn Diesen"),  talking about the Russian war and how it was in part caused my Western expansionism. Without excusing Russia's actions in the slightest, we can the West (led by the US), believing that its ideology is best, has no problem with toppling governments that don't agree and imposing their beliefs with impunity. Instead of leaning on diplomacy and distributing power when needed, the West is simply playing the game for itself and its own interests (not unlike Russia).

In his talk [The Democratic Frame of Mind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sAxL_Zep2D0&ab_channel=FreudMuseumLondon), Christopher Bollas discusses how democracy is not voting for a leader every few years, but about how we relate to each other, and ourselves. He talks about how we have different selves within our mind, with different ideas and perceptions of the world, but we understand them all to be ourselves, representations of the 'I', not 'the other'. In the same way, people with different voices and understandings of the world are not 'the other', just different representations of the 'we.'

Importantly, he mentions that in politics, we are not interested in incorporating psychology, or diplomacy as an extension, as we can see reflected in the West's actions in Ukraine. We don't want to apologize or listen to other people's point of view, it's all about imposing winning ideas. He talks about doing politics differently, as a group process whereby everything anyone comes together to share their points of view rather than debate each other, and how that allows for empathy and truly coming together of minds and experiences.

This is closer to Nora Bateson's Warm Data Labs than the kind of democracy we have now. In our current system, it's not about making good or right decisions, it's about convincing people to follow the elites. Propaganda is more common in democracies than in authoritarian systems, because authoritarian leaders have a lesser need to convince people they are right. Through propaganda, democracy becomes about bringing people over to your side through a moralized narrative, rather than about working together to create a society that works for everyone.

What does this say about the need for cultural evolution? More than ever, we need our choice- and decision-making systems to be about truly finding the right path, and building up human capacity on the way, not about 'convincing people.' This isn't just about building new tools, it's about shifting the way we relate to one another.

So how do we do this? I think that physical hubs for learning and coming together are going to be more important than ever. Wether it's bioregional learning centers, commons hubs, philosophy clubs, democracy centers, symbiotic networks or community centers, we need to start building spaces where we can experiment with new ways of coming together. Through these third spaces we can start to build communities that hold deep conversations other over time, rather than being swayed by cultural narratives imposed by elites.
