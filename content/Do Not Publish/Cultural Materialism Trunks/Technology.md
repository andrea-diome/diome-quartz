## What is technology?
**Technology is the act of structuring of materials, energy, information to adapt & thrive.** 
At its core, technology is how we act on the world. Technology is not limited to physical objects or devices; it also includes techniques, processes, and systems that enable humans to interact with and shape their surroundings. 

Most of our interactions with planetary ecosystems are mediated by technology. For example, you might be sitting in your house right now. In that case, your house is a technological infrastructure by which you're currently interacting with the planetary ecosystem.

The interplay between materials, energy, and information is crucial to the development and implementation of technology. All technologies combine or are made possible by a combination of material, energy & information.

## Why is technology important?
> *Technologies always create the potential for new forms of behavior, values, and thought, even when the technology is not explicitly made to do so.* [Technology is Not Values Neutral: Ending the Reign of Nihilistic Design](https://consilienceproject.org/technology-is-not-values-neutral)

**Technologies determine how we organize our society.**
The way we design, develop, and deploy technologies can either reinforce existing power structures and inequalities or create opportunities for more equitable and sustainable futures. By recognizing the inherent value-laden nature of technology, we can work towards creating technologies that align with our desired outcomes and contribute to the well-being of both humans and the planet.

Now more than ever, we are building exponential technologies that have the potential to transform our world in profound ways. However, the driving force behind technological development is often money, rather than ethics or the greater good. If we don't find a way to reconcile our technological abilities with social and natural capital, we risk nothing less than the collapse of our civilization.
### Why are information technologies particularly important?
> _Technologies are not mere exterior aids, but also interior transformations of consciousness, and never more than when they affect the world._ [Walter Ong](https://www.jstor.org/stable/43501355).

Information technologies are particularly important because they directly shape how we process, share, and make sense of the world. These technologies encompass a wide range of tools and systems, from the invention of writing and the printed word to the rise of markets, digital communication networks and artificial intelligence. 

The information technology [underpinning a society](https://www.radicalxchange.org/media/blog/towards-decentralized-civic-infrastructure/) profoundly impacts its structure and outcomes. For example, the development of land surveying and cartography in 17th century Europe transformed land into bounded commodities and enabled the concept of state jurisdiction through geographical territory. Similarly, the logistical challenges of counting paper ballots have led democracies to rely on representatives elected on multi-annual cycles.
## How has technology impacted civilizations throughout history?
### Indigenous
- The reliance on agroforestry, hunting, and gathering for subsistence necessitates a deep understanding of local ecosystems, land management practices closely tied to kinship networks and a worldview emphasizing the interconnectedness of all life.
- The limited accumulation of surplus and the emphasis on sharing and reciprocity favors egalitarian social relations.
- The development of rituals, ceremonies, and spiritual practices around key ecological events, such as planting, harvesting, and hunting, encodes and transmits practical knowledge and cultural values across generations.
- The use of oral traditions, such as storytelling, songs, and myths, as a primary means of information storage and transmission promotes a holistic, and context-dependent understanding and responsive of the world grounded in lived experience and social relationships.
### Agro-Pastoral
- The domestication of plants and animals as dominant modes of subsistence shaped a worldview that emphasized the control and manipulation of nature for human benefit and the importance of fertility, cycles, and order in the cosmic and social realms.
- The need to coordinate labor and resources for large-scale agriculture and the construction of infrastructure, such as irrigation systems and granaries, led to the emergence of hierarchical and centralized forms of social organization, such as monarchies and bureaucracies, as well as the specialization of labor and the division of society into distinct classes, such as peasants, artisans, and elites.
- The development of trade networks and markets for the exchange of agricultural surpluses and craft goods fostered the growth of merchant and financial classes and the integration of different regions and cultures into larger economic and political units.
- The need for record-keeping of agro-pastoral production led to the development of writing systems and emergence of literate elites, such as scribes and priests, who played a key role in the production and transmission of cultural knowledge and ideology.
- The construction of monumental architecture, such as temples, palaces, and tombs, served to legitimize the power and authority of ruling elites and to encode cultural values and beliefs in tangible and enduring forms.
### Industrial
- The development of mechanized production, factory systems, and the use of fossil fuels as energy sources led to the concentration of economic power in the hands of industrial capitalists and the emergence of new social classes, such as industrial workers and a professional-managerial class.
- The need to support industrial production and trade fostered the need for large-scale transportation and communication networks leading to the expansion of bureaucratic and centralized forms of social organization, such as the nation-state and the corporation.
- The separation of home and workplace led to the emergence of new forms of domestic life and gender roles, such as the nuclear family and the cult of domesticity, which were closely tied to the demands of industrial production and consumption.
- The development of mass production and consumption as dominant modes of economic organization shaped a social order based on individualism, competition, and the pursuit of material prosperity and social status.
- The development of print media, mass education, and new forms of cultural production, in conjunction with the growth of industrial capitalism, led to the emergence of new forms of public discourse and the formation of national cultures and identities.
- The use of science and technology as key drivers of economic and social progress shaped a worldview that emphasized the power of human reason and the ability to control and manipulate the natural world for human benefit.
## How can technology support the transition to a Regenerative Civilization?
To transition to a Regenerative Civilization, we need to redefine our [[1.3.1 Superstructure]], [[1.3.2 Social Structure]], and [[1.3.3 Infrastructure]]. While technology alone is not the ultimate answer, it plays a significant role in shaping our social and superstructures, so it needs to be part of the solution. By redesigning the technologies of cultural production and governance, we may be able to chart a path towards a more sustainable and equitable future.
