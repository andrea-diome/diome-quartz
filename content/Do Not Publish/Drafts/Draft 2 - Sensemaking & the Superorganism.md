## Sensemaking
Do you remember the moment you truly saw the world for the first time? For me, it happened at 14 when I put in my first contact lenses. Suddenly, trees weren't just green blurs but intricate networks of swaying leaves, and cars seemed to jump out of a pop-up book.

I'd always needed glasses but refused to wear them, causing my vision to deteriorate, especially in my left eye. This disparity affected a process called stereopsis - the brain's ability to create 3D perception from slightly different images received by each eye. Without glasses, I had little depth perception, as if seeing the world in photographs. Putting on contact lenses was like stepping into a new dimension. 

Stereopsis is sensemaking in action: combining two distinct images to unlock a superpower - experiencing another dimension. Imagine suddenly seeing an extra dimension, as if magic glasses allowed you to perceive all of time: past, present, and future. That's the power of sensemaking: integrating diverse inputs to construct a richer understanding of reality. 

Sensemaking is a crucial step before action. It's how we transform the raw data of our experiences into the understanding that guides our decisions and behaviors. The better we can make sense of data to create a multi-dimensional picture of the world, the better our decisions and subsequent actions.

## The Superorganism
We are beginning to understand human civilization as an emerging planetary superorganism. A superorganism is an entity comprised of many individual organisms working together as a unified, complex system, like an ant colony or bee hive. In this context, humanity and its technological extensions form a global network that exhibits behaviors and capabilities beyond those of its individual components.

Power grids and energy distribution networks function like the metabolic processes of a living organism, providing the necessary energy for various functions across the planet. Our global trade networks act as a circulatory system, moving resources and information around the world. The internet serves as the nervous system of this emerging global entity, with social media platforms, search engines, and other digital technologies acting as the cognitive tools that allow this collective intelligence to process information and solve problems.

==this reflects== In the 1970s, James Lovelock proposed the Gaia hypothesis, suggesting that our planet functions as a self-regulating, complex  living organism. This revolutionary concept posits that the biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere are intricately linked, working together to maintain the conditions necessary for life.

## Planetary Sapience

Collective intelligence refers to the enhanced cognitive and problem-solving capabilities that emerge when individuals work together. It allows us to tackle complex, global challenges that are beyond the scope of any individual or small group. Collective intelligence is, in many ways, an output of collective sensemaking.

- **Democratic Voting**: While imperfect, voting systems aggregate the opinions and preferences of large populations to make collective decisions.
- **Open-source Software**: Projects like Linux involve thousands of developers collaborating to create complex systems.
- **Citizen Science**: Platforms like Zooniverse allow ordinary people to contribute to scientific research on a massive scale.
- **Prediction Markets**: These aggregate the judgments of many individuals to forecast future events, often with surprising accuracy.
