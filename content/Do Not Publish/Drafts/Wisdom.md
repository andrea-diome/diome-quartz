## Deep Rationality Theory (DRT)
This theory proposes wisdom as a form of deep, comprehensive rationality. According to DRT, a wise person should have:

1. Justified beliefs across various academic subjects: A wise person possesses well-reasoned beliefs about a wide range of topics, including science, history, philosophy, and other academic disciplines. These beliefs are not necessarily absolute knowledge, but rather well-supported understandings based on evidence and logical reasoning.
2. Justified beliefs about rational living: This involves a deep understanding of how to live rationally in three primary domains: 
	a) Epistemology: The wise person knows how to form and evaluate beliefs effectively, using critical thinking and evidence-based reasoning. 
	b) Morality: They have a well-developed ethical framework and understand how to act ethically in various situations. 
	c) Practicality: The wise individual knows how to navigate daily life efficiently and effectively, making good decisions to achieve their goals.
1. Commitment to living rationally: Wisdom isn't just about understanding rationality; it requires a dedication to applying this understanding in one's life. A wise person consistently strives to make rational decisions and act in accordance with their well-reasoned beliefs.
2. Few unjustified beliefs and awareness of limitations: A key aspect of wisdom is epistemic humility. The wise person is cautious about forming beliefs without sufficient evidence or reasoning. They also recognize the limits of their own knowledge and abilities, understanding that there is always more to learn and that their current beliefs may be subject to revision in light of new information.
