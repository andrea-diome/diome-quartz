
**2. The Essence of Money**

**2.1. Historical Context**: From bartering goods to minting coins, to paper currency, and now digital representations, money has seen various incarnations.

**2.2. Functions**: Money primarily serves as:

- A medium of exchange, eliminating the need for a coincidence of wants.
- A unit of account, providing a consistent measure of value.
- A store of value, preserving wealth for future use.


**5. Why Do We Use Money?**

**5.1. Simplifying Trade**: Money removes the inefficiencies of barter, making transactions smoother and more standardized.

**5.2. Delayed Gratification**: Money allows us to store value and make deferred payments.

**5.3. Economic Measurement**: Money provides a unit to measure and compare the cost of goods and services.


**2. Money as Sensemaking**

**2.1. Symbolic Representation**: Money symbolizes a promise, an expectation of value. It translates abstract worth into a tangible form that can be universally understood within a given society.

**2.2. Structuring Societal Understanding**: By establishing what can be acquired or owed, money sets boundaries, determines desirability, and influences decision-making.

**2.3. Influencing Behavior**: As a sensemaking tool, money directs actions, from individual consumption patterns to collective investment and societal priorities.

---

**3. Money as Communication**

**3.1. Conveying Worth**: The act of spending or saving communicates one's valuation of an item or service.

**3.2. Reflecting Societal Values**: The value ascribed to various professions, goods, or services speaks volumes about societal priorities and beliefs.

**3.3. Facilitating Interpersonal Interactions**: Monetary transactions, whether salaries, gifts, or donations, inherently involve interpersonal communications and negotiations of worth.
