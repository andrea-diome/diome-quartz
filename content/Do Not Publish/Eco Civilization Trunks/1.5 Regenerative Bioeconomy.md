## What is it?
Just like a biological organism, societies take in resources, transform them, and output waste. In healthy organisms, metabolic processes integrate into the larger ecosystem, with the waste from one process becoming the input for another.

In the complex organism of human society, economic systems determine how we produce, distribute, and consume goods and services. These decisions are influenced by factors including social structures and institutions, consumer demands, available resources, and the prevailing values of the society.

## What are the key problems?
Our current economic system is defined by 3 key concepts, sometimes referred to as The Ring of Power: [Accumulation, Extraction & Abstraction](https://diome.xyz/4+%F0%9F%8C%B0+Seeds/Accumulation%2C+Extraction+%26+Abstraction). In our current system, profit is created by extracting as much value from nature and other people as possible, and accumulating that value rather than replenishing it. Abstraction happens when a resource is extracted from its environment and transformed into a more accumulable and exchangeable form, like money. Money is power because, although it doesn't have intrinsic value, it can be exchanged for anything, whereas resources or ecosystem actors have value only in context.

Equating money to value means that only what is extracted from the system is seen, without accounting for [Externalities](https://diome.xyz/2+%F0%9F%8C%BF+Leaves/Externalities), the effects of those actions. Ease of accumulation incentivizes extraction beyond the replenishment rate of the system, while reducing the number of resources in circulation. This ultimately leads to a decay in non-financial [Forms Of Capital](https://diome.xyz/2+%F0%9F%8C%BF+Leaves/Forms+Of+Capital), leaving society to bear the costs of degenerated and fragile eco- and social systems. Some examples of this dynamic are:

- We use chemical fertilizers to monocrop produce, depleting the soil, producing increasingly less nutritious foods, and turning agriculture into one of the biggest carbon emitting industries, while [biochemical manufacturers](https://theconversation.com/the-green-revolution-is-a-warning-not-a-blueprint-for-feeding-a-hungry-planet-182269) and agriculture corporates profit.
- We mass produce everything from textiles to medical supplies to electrical products in hard-to-trace, just-in-time global supply chains, making production systems highly efficient at extracting profits, while impoverishing and exploiting communities, heavily polluting ecosystems and, as COVID-19 showed, making essential industries highly fragile.
- Our social platforms profit from human interaction, reducing connection to what can be monetized, transforming communication into a polarized boxing match, endangering both individual humans and society-scale governance systems.

This cycle is enabled by social structures around ownership, agency and responsibility. Because extraction ultimately leads to power, those who can best extract gain ownership over property and agency over what gets produced without the responsibility to maintain the ecosystem that sustains it. This separates local communities from the resources they need to be self-sufficient, making them dependent on extraction while at the same time bearing the brunt of its effects.

## What solution hypotheses do we have?
The [Regenerative Economy](https://diome.xyz/2+%F0%9F%8C%BF+Leaves/Regenerative+Economy) must act as a healthy life-sustaining metabolism, based on the principles of [Contextualization, Instantiation & Distribution](https://diome.xyz/4+%F0%9F%8C%B0+Seeds/Contextualization%2C+Instantiation+%26+Distribution). Contextualization harnesses complex systemic interdependencies inherent in living systems to generate non-fungible value situated within its unique ecosystem. Concepts of circular or [bioeconomies](https://www.metabolic.nl/news/how-to-create-a-sustainable-bioeconomy) are based primarily on this principle.

Instantiation means focusing economics on directly improving the tangible, experiential fundamentals of human life and ecology, rather than increasing financial wealth as an end itself. Broadly, this would mean moving away from optimizing measures such as profit and GPD, and towards improving impact, health and happiness. Concepts of the care economy are based primarily on this principle.

Distribution means utilizing the potential abundance enabled by technology to circulate resources in equitable, efficient flows, rather than accumulating for private gain. Rather than creating false scarcity, it means carefully managing truly ecologically scarce resources. Value flows are like water: they bring life when its circulating but stagnate when standing still. Concepts of UBI are based primarily on this principle.
