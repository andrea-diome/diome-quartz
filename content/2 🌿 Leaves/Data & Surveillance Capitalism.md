An internet built on the mechanistic view of the world has created a manipulation machine, in which private companies control our tools of identity creation and community collaboration.  It’s not surprising that data is so often called ‘the new oil.’ Just as the environmental and human consequences of oil extraction have been deemed an inevitable price to pay for ‘progress’:

> "*Capitalism today is driven by the assumption that access to masses of data is a business and economic imperative. Human experience, potentially every layer and aspect of it, is becoming the target of profitable extraction."

## The Data Exploitation Loop
In this model, human experience is turned into behavioural data through quantification. This behavioural data is then passed through machine learning algorithms in order to predict our future behaviour, and how it might be moulded. Advertisers then purchase these predictions in the form of ad views or conversions, resulting in profitable outcomes for both platforms and advertisers.

> *Anytime anyone connects online, it's financed by another person who believes they can manipulate the first two.* [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KNOlqzMd2Zw&ab_channel=UCSantaCruzArts%2CLectures%2CandEntertainment)

> "Data is monetized when it is sold and resold, and algorithms are used to aggregate data from different sources to build an increasingly detailed picture of personal habits and preferences, which companies feed into predictive tools that model future outcomes and produce categorizations, scores, and rankings."
> [[Data Capitalism and Algorithmic Racism]]

This model has several problems, including:
- The platform's profit depends on you being on the platform as much as possible in order to keep making profit. Therefore, they have to draw your attention with incendiary posts to keep you ‘engaged’. This benefits people with the most horrible views and makes their point of view more and more mainstream. Real conversation or communication is not important here. Just like measures of GDP which consider any spending good regardless of what the money is spent on, so is engagement a one-dimensional figure. Therefore, connection itself is one-dimensional. (Related to: [[Social Structure]])
- Commodification or personal brandification of everyone, and the phenomenon of people changing to suit their audiences. Identity is mediated by the platforms, therefore we're not allowed to have complex identities that are always changing. (Related to:[[Metacrisis Series by GreenPill Podcast]], [[How To Do Nothing - Resisting the Attention Economy]])
- The inability to have conversations in the private realm. Social change happens by the slow communication with groups of increasingly growing size. Also, it needs real reflection and the ability to make long, complex arguments. Social media makes this very difficult. (Related to: [[How To Do Nothing - Resisting the Attention Economy]])

All this together means that we are stuck in a loop, unable to create any real change and supporting the polarisation of our society.
## History of Data Capitalism
> *The spirit there was very decentralized. The individual was incredibly empowered. It was all based on there being no central authority that you had to go to to ask permission.*
> [Tim Berners Lee](https://www.vanityfair.com/news/2018/07/the-man-who-created-the-world-wide-web-has-some-regrets), one of the creators of the internet.

Ironically, Jason Lanier argues that the over-idealization of the free and open internet, without questioning the underlying social structures, is one of the reasons why it went so wrong in the first place. 

In the 90s there was the so-called dot-com boom: when investors got over excited about the promise of the internet and threw money at e-startups left, right and center. But all that goes up must come down, and soon enough the bubble burst when everyone realized that these companies had no idea how to make profit. 

The pressure was put on to find a viable business model. But, how do you make money without going up against the 'free and open' ideology of the early internet? Enter Google: they had the crazy realization that they were collecting lots of data about their users, more than they really needed to provide their services, and that maybe that could be valuable. The targeted advertising model was born.
